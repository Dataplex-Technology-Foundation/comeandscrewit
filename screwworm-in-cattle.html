Screwworm in cattle and livestock

For a cattle operation, screwworm is a working threat, not a curiosity. Any animal with an open wound is a target, and newborn calves — with a fresh navel — are especially vulnerable.

Signs to look for

  • A wound that grows instead of heals.
  • Draining or foul-smelling discharge.
  • Visible maggots deep in the wound.
  • Animals that are restless, off feed, isolating, or headshaking (if the infestation is in the head or nose).

Check high-risk sites first: navels of newborns, castration and branding wounds, tick bites, dehorning sites, and any cut or sore.

What to do if you suspect it

Do not wait. Report immediately to your state animal health authority — in Texas, your TAHC area office or your USDA area veterinarian in charge. Isolate the animal if practical.

Full reporting steps and contacts are on Take Action.

Prevention

  • Inspect stock regularly — especially after any procedure that breaks skin.
  • Time husbandry procedures to lower-risk windows where possible.
  • Treat and cover wounds.
  • Watch newborns closely, particularly the navel.
