Texas & New Mexico

Ranchers: get the early warning you need

If you run livestock in Texas or New Mexico, screwworm is already at your door. Here's how to protect your operation now — and how to get early access to what we're building.

Report a suspected case (do this first)

Texas: contact your TAHC area office or USDA area veterinarian in charge immediately.

New Mexico: Contact the New Mexico Livestock Board.

What to report: wounds that enlarge or drain, visible maggots, animals in distress. Full steps → Take Action.

Protect your herd now

  • Inspect after any wound-causing procedure (castration, branding, dehorning).
  • Watch newborn navels closely.
  • Treat and cover wounds.
  • Check high-risk sites: navels, tick bites, cuts, sores.

Why it's worth it

Early detection means faster sterile-fly coverage, tighter quarantines, and fewer losses in your area.
